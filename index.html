About the Graduate Program

The Master of Science in Applied Mathematics and Computational Science (MSc in AMCS) degree is intended to provide the student a working knowledge of several areas of applied mathematics, which may include a specific area of application, to prepare for a dynamic career in industry and academia. In addition to providing a solid conceptual foundation for the applications of mathematics, students will be prepared to complete a thesis in topics related to Applied Mathematics and Computational Science.

Vision & Mission

Our vision is to develop and enhance students' understanding and skill by offering advanced education and research training in applied mathematics. To achieve this, we aim to:

  • Educate students to meet high standards of excellence in applied mathematics.
  • Foster skilled applied mathematicians with advanced computing knowledge.
  • Create and disseminate knowledge through applied research.

Graduate Attributes

Upon graduation, students will be equipped to be active and engaged global citizens who can make a significant impact in their chosen fields.

  • Be equipped with comprehensive knowledge and the ability to apply it in practice, including in multi-disciplinary contexts.
  • Exhibit well-developed skills in critical and creative thinking, and engage in innovative research.
  • Be well-prepared for living, learning, and working in a digital society.
  • Convey complex ideas effectively and contribute positively in collaborative environments.
  • Become responsible and effective global citizens, consistent with their roles as members of society.

Program Educational Objectives (PEOs)

Graduates of the AMCS program will be able to:

PEO1: Excel as Professionals

Build upon problem-solving skills, teamwork abilities, and communication skills in fields concerned with applied mathematics.

PEO2: Competent Computational Skills

Analyze and illustrate complex mathematical models related to real-life problems and make informed decisions.

PEO3: Lifelong Learning

Participate in activities that enhance professional development through graduate studies, training, and experiences.

PEO4: Ethical Leadership

Demonstrate globally recognized social justice, ethical roles, and responsibilities through contributions to society.

Admission Requirements

  • A 4-year bachelor's degree in a relevant field (Mathematics, Statistics, Physics, Engineering, CSE, Economics, etc.) with a minimum CGPA of 2.75.
  • A 3-year bachelor's degree may be considered, conditional on completing 9 credits of remedial mathematics courses.
  • Acceptable score in the NSU-administered Admission Test or a GRE General Test score of 1100 (Quantitative + Verbal).
  • Two letters of recommendation.
  • Prerequisite undergraduate courses: Calculus (Single & Multivariable), Linear Algebra, and Differential Equations.

Course Details

Core Courses (15 Credit Hours)

All courses are mandatory and form the foundation of the AMCS program.

AMCS 501: Computational Linear Algebra

Focuses on matrix computations, algorithms, and their applications in solving large-scale problems.

AMCS 501L: Computational Linear Algebra Lab

Hands-on implementation of numerical linear algebra algorithms using modern software tools.

AMCS 502: High Performance Parallel Computing

Explores parallel computing architectures and programming models for scientific and engineering applications.

AMCS 502L: High Performance Parallel Computing Lab

Practical experience in developing and optimizing parallel programs for multi-core systems.

AMCS 503: Partial Differential Equations: Modeling and Numerics

Covers the theory of PDEs, modeling real-world phenomena, and numerical solution techniques.

AMCS 504: Advanced Numerical Methods and Computation

Advanced study of numerical techniques for approximation, integration, and solving differential equations.

AMCS 504L: Advanced Numerical Methods and Computation Lab

Implementation of complex numerical methods for solving advanced mathematical problems.

Elective Courses (6 Credit Hours)

Select any two courses to specialize your knowledge.

AMCS 505: Advanced Control Theory and Application

Studies modern control systems, including state-space analysis, optimal control, and stability.

AMCS 506: Computational Fluid Dynamics

Introduces numerical methods for simulating fluid flow and heat transfer problems.

AMCS 507: Advanced Mathematical Modelling

Develops skills in creating, analyzing, and interpreting mathematical models from various scientific fields.

Optional Courses (9 Credit Hours)

Choose any three courses to further broaden your expertise.

AMCS 508: Dynamical Systems and Chaos

An introduction to the behavior of nonlinear systems, bifurcations, and chaotic dynamics.

AMCS 509: Mathematics for Data Science and Machine Learning

Covers the core mathematical concepts that underpin modern data science and ML algorithms.

AMCS 510: Mathematical Control Theory

A rigorous study of the mathematical foundations of control theory for dynamic systems.

AMCS 511: Operations Research

Focuses on mathematical optimization techniques for decision-making in complex systems.

AMCS 512: Numerical Analysis

A foundational course on the theory and application of numerical algorithms.

AMCS 513: Financial Mathematics

Explores mathematical models used in finance, including stochastic calculus and option pricing.

AMCS 514: Applied Harmonic Analysis

Studies the theory and applications of Fourier analysis, wavelets, and signal processing.

AMCS 515: Essential of Advanced Mathematics - I

A refresher course on advanced topics in calculus, algebra, and analysis for students from diverse backgrounds.

AMCS 516: Essential of Advanced Mathematics - II

A continuation of AMCS 515, covering more advanced mathematical concepts required for the program.

GED Course (4 Credit Hours)

AMCS 517: Research Methodology in Mathematics Education

Introduces research methods, academic writing, and presentation skills essential for graduate-level research.

AMCS 517L: Labs on Mathematical Research Tools

Practical training on using software like LaTeX, MATLAB, or Python for mathematical research and documentation.

Graduate Thesis (6 Credit Hours)

AMCS 599: Graduate Thesis

A mandatory, two-semester research project where students conduct an in-depth study on a topic in applied mathematics or computational science under the supervision of a faculty member. This culminates in a written thesis and a final defense.
